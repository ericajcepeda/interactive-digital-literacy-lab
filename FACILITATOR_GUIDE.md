# R.B.'s Bat Lab Facilitator Guide

## Purpose

This guide is intended for facilitators, instructors, judges, or staff supporting participants as they complete R.B.'s Bat Lab.

R.B.'s Bat Lab is an interactive digital literacy challenge that introduces participants to coding, cybersecurity, data analysis, AI and digital communication, file organization, and password security.

The participant-facing challenge is available here:

[Launch R.B.'s Bat Lab](https://sites.google.com/view/csitchallenge/home)

## Facilitator Notes

Participants should be encouraged to work through each activity independently before receiving an answer.

If a participant becomes stuck, provide a hint before giving the full solution.

The challenge includes simulated login, authentication, email, and password interfaces. Participants should never enter their actual usernames, passwords, or other credentials.

# Part 2: Cyber Lab

## Learning Focus

This activity introduces participants to common phishing indicators.

Participants compare two simulated emails and determine which one contains signs of phishing.

## Legitimate Email

Email #1 is the legitimate message.

It appears to come from:

`sl@austincc.edu`

The message contains information about an ACC Student Life event.

## Phishing Email

Email #2 is the phishing attempt.

The message includes several warning signs:

1. Suspicious sender domain: `acc-securityverify.info`
2. Urgent language claiming the password will expire immediately
3. A threat that the account will be deactivated
4. A suspicious verification link: `acc-verify-reset.info/login`
5. Spelling and formatting errors, including `Highlind` and `Supp0rt`

**Number of red flags: 5**

## Part 2 Verification

**Answer: 5**

Facilitator discussion can focus on why urgency, unfamiliar domains, suspicious links, spelling errors, and threats should cause users to stop and verify a message before acting.

# Final Security Step: Password Reset

## Learning Focus

The final activity reinforces strong password and passphrase practices.

Participants must construct a passphrase that:

- Contains at least four tokens
- Includes at least one number
- Includes at least one special character
- Does not contain one of the banned weak-password terms

The accepted special characters are:

`!` `#` `@` `_`

The challenge rejects passphrases containing:

- `password`
- `riverbat`
- `acc123`

There is not one required password answer. Any combination that satisfies all of the requirements can complete the activity.

# Suggested Facilitation Approach

Facilitators should avoid immediately providing the answer when a participant becomes stuck.

A recommended progression is:

1. Ask the participant to reread the clue.
2. Direct attention to the relevant information on the screen.
3. Ask a guiding question.
4. Provide a stronger hint if needed.
5. Give the answer only when necessary to keep the participant moving.

The goal of the experience is not simply to reach the final screen. Participants should have opportunities to identify why an answer is correct and connect each activity to a practical digital skill.


# Troubleshooting

## Participant cannot log in

Confirm that the participant is using the fictional Bat Lab credentials and has not entered a real ACC username or password.

## MFA does not work

The correct simulation code is `1973`.

## Part 1 will not advance

The final cipher shift entered in the verification field must be `3`.

## Part 2 will not advance

The phishing red flag count must be `5`.

## Part 3 will not advance

All three selections must be:

1. Joy
2. Joy
3. San Gabriel Campus

## Part 4 will not advance

Participants should complete Byte's conversation and enter `10` as the Message Lab number.

## File organization will not advance

Confirm that every file has been placed and that each file is in its correct folder.

## Final lock will not open

The final total is `18`.

## Password activity will not complete

Confirm that the participant has selected at least four tokens, including a number and special character, without using one of the banned password terms.
